# Reviewing a card

For the clinician or pharmacist who clears cards. You are the last human between the
research and someone treating patients. This should take five to ten minutes per card.

## What you are accountable for

Not whether the card is well written. Whether a provider acting on it would be misled.

You need **Editor** access to the dashboard. With it you see a **Review queue** under the
card area; staff do not, and staff cannot read the drafts in it.

## How to work a draft

Open it from the queue. You get the card exactly as staff would see it, plus the fact-check
report beneath it.

Read the report first, then the card. The report is organized so you can triage:

- **Blocking defects** — red. Each names the claim as written, what the source actually says,
  and the source that was opened. If any exist, the clear button is disabled.
- **Fixes required** — amber. Real but not dangerous. Your call whether to clear with the fix
  applied or send it back.
- **Could not verify** — anything the checker could not run to a primary source. Each one is
  at minimum a fix: unverifiable content comes off the card or gets labelled.
- **Verified** — what was actually checked. Read this to see what the check *covered*, which
  tells you where your own attention is still needed.

Then read the card with a provider's eyes. The checker verifies provenance; it does not
know your patient population or your formulary.

## Clear it or send it back

**Clear for staff** writes the card to the cleared collection with your name and today's
date. Every open dashboard picks it up immediately. There is no second review after yours.

**Send back** returns it with a note. Write the note for the person who will fix it: name
the claim and what is wrong with it. "Drug table is off" costs another round trip.

## Send it back if any of these is true

- A drug is listed without its boxed warning, or the warning is incomplete.
- A guideline is cited without its year, or the version cited has been superseded.
- A claim is not supported by the source attached to it.
- Any row reads as advice for an individual patient rather than what the label or guideline
  says.
- A dose, date, trial ID or drug name looks wrong, even slightly.
- The clinical-reference footer is missing.
- Something important for your population is absent and not named in "Not on this card."

## Two things to resist

**Clearing because the card is otherwise good.** The card ships as a whole. A provider
cannot see which row you had doubts about.

**Rewriting the card yourself.** The queue is for clearing and returning. If you fix a card
in place, nobody reviews your fix — which is the problem you were hired to prevent.

## A clean pass is a real outcome

If the report is clean and the card reads correctly, clear it. Do not manufacture findings
to look thorough; that trains everyone downstream to skim your judgments, and the one time
it matters they will skim past something real.
