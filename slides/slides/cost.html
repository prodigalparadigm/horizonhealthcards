Every number here is measured from a real run, not modelled. That is the point of showing the table rather than a single figure. The one assumption: the harness reports total tokens per agent, not the input/output split. These figures assume 92 percent input, which is typical for agentic loops because context and tool results are resent every turn. At 95/5 the total is $0.69; at 85/15 it is $0.91. The answer is stable either way. The fact-check line is the one to defend. It runs on the most capable model and it opens every source. That is where the money should sit, and the previous slide is the evidence. If asked about volume discounts or batching: prompt caching reduces the orchestration half across a batch, which is why the all-in figure drops as waves get larger.
