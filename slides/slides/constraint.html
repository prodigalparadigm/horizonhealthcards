Compute is not the constraint

The whole 200-condition catalog, carded and checked:

Model spend

$300

A rounding error against a single service-line decision.

Clinician review

17–33 hrs

Fifteen to twenty times the model spend, in the scarcest time you have.

The first card needed a correction pass, so budget one and a half to two review passes per card, not one. And a queue of 200 unreviewed cards creates pressure to rubber-stamp, which is the single failure this system exists to prevent.

Review hours from 5 to 10 minutes per card. Apply your own loaded reviewer rate.

This is the decision slide. The instinct in the room will be to ask what the AI costs. The answer is that it costs almost nothing and that is not the interesting number. Three hundred dollars buys the entire catalog. Twenty-five hours of a pharmacist or physician is the actual price, and it is the thing to plan around. Deliberately no salary assumption here - give them the hours and let them apply their own loaded rate. If pressed, review labour lands somewhere around fifteen to twenty times model spend. The bottom box is the honest part. One card is not a rate, but the first one needed rework, and planning for a single clean pass per card would be optimistic. The rubber-stamp point is worth saying out loud. A reviewer facing twelve items behaves differently from one facing two hundred. That is why the next slide proposes waves rather than a bulk run.
