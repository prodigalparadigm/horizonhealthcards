# Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus — Adult, Non-Pregnant (E11.9)

*As of 2026-10-12. Draft — pending factchecker review.*

## Quick Summary

Chronic hyperglycemia from insulin resistance with relative insulin deficiency; most adult
cases, strongly linked to obesity/metabolic risk. Often asymptomatic at diagnosis (found on
screening); symptomatic presentation includes polyuria, polydipsia, fatigue, blurred vision.
ADA 2026 frames GLP-1 RA and SGLT2i therapy as disease-modifying — considered from diagnosis,
with strength of recommendation scaling to ASCVD/HF/CKD risk — layered on lifestyle change and
metformin, rather than reserved as later add-ons. (ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026, Sec. 9)

## Quick News Update

- **2026-08** — Mounjaro (tirzepatide) FDA-approved to reduce MACE risk in adults with T2D at
  high CV risk; SURPASS-CVOT (~13,000 pts, >4.5 yr) showed noninferiority to dulaglutide on
  MACE-3 (HR 0.92, 95.3% CI 0.83–1.01) — first dual GIP/GLP-1 with this claim. [Sponsor release, Tier 3](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fda-approves-lillys-mounjaro-tirzepatide-reduce-cardiovascular)
- **2026-03** — Awiqli (insulin icodec) FDA-approved; first once-weekly basal insulin for
  adults with T2DM, reducing basal injections from 7/week to 1 (ONWARDS program, ~2,680 pts). [Label, DailyMed](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5162b93e-5775-4581-a5e9-e8783ae0acd7)
- **2025-10** — Rybelsus (oral semaglutide) FDA-approved for MACE risk reduction in adults
  with T2D at high CV risk, regardless of prior CV event — first oral GLP-1 RA with a MACE
  indication (SOUL trial, n=9,650, ~14% relative MACE reduction over ~4 yr). [Sponsor release, Tier 3](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fda-approves-novo-nordisks-oral-semaglutide-for-cardiovascular-cv-risk-reduction-in-adults-with-type-2-diabetes-who-are-at-high-risk-including-those-who-have-not-had-a-prior-cv-event-302588022.html)
- **2025** — Of 5,783 newly diagnosed adults across 60 West Michigan primary-care clinics
  (Apr 2021–Jan 2023), only 19.9% were prescribed an SGLT2i or GLP-1 RA within 3 months — rate
  barely higher in guideline-qualifying subgroups (ischemic heart disease 20.0%, eGFR<60 19.3%,
  BMI≥32 20.3%), suggesting a persistent gap between guideline recommendation and practice. [J Diabetes Res 2025;2025:6656982](https://doi.org/10.1155/jdr/6656982)

Guideline note (not a label fact): do not combine a DPP-4 inhibitor with a GLP-1 RA or dual
GIP/GLP-1 — no added glucose-lowering benefit (ADA 2026). On insulin already — add a GLP-1 RA
rather than intensifying insulin alone (ADA 2026).
