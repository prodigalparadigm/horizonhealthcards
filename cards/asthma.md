# Asthma — Adult and Adolescent (J45.909)

*As of 2026-10-12. Draft — pending factchecker review.*

## Quick Summary

Chronic airway inflammation causing episodic wheeze, cough, dyspnea, and chest tightness from
variable, reversible airflow obstruction; affects adolescents and adults, often atopic. Managed
with stepped inhaled controller ± reliever therapy, escalated for symptom control and
exacerbation risk, with biologic add-on at the top step for refractory disease.

**Reliever strategy — the guidelines disagree.** GINA 2025's preferred Track 1 uses as-needed
low-dose ICS-formoterol as both maintenance and reliever (MART) across Steps 1–5 (ages ≥12)
and no longer prefers SABA-alone as reliever at any step. US NAEPP 2020 — still the current
federal guideline; no newer edition exists as of Oct 2026 — permits daily ICS + as-needed SABA
as an equal Step 2 option, and only moves to ICS-formoterol-as-reliever (SMART) from Step 3
(moderate-to-severe persistent, ages ≥4) up, not down to mild disease like GINA. NAEPP also
warns ICS-formoterol must never be used as a reliever on top of ICS-salmeterol maintenance.
Biologic eligibility converges at Step 5 (failed high-dose ICS-LABA); phenotype, not line,
differentiates among agents.

## Quick News Update

- **2025-11-14** — GINA 2025 received a minor correction (pediatric nebulized salbutamol
  dose only; Track 1 MART strategy unchanged from May 2025). [GINA](https://ginasthma.org/minor-update-to-gina-2025-report-and-good-news-about-access-to-gina-resources/)

  reduction in mild asthma. Company-sourced; Tier 3. [Sponsor source, Tier 3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e60aa9f9-5139-44df-b886-ebddc3c7eb13)
- **2025-03-07** — FDA approved Omlyclo (omalizumab-igec), first omalizumab biosimilar and
  first respiratory biosimilar in the US, interchangeable; same anaphylaxis boxed warning
  as reference Xolair. [Label, FDA](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/761399s000lbl.pdf)

## Not on this card

- US asthma prevalence — not pulled from a primary CDC source this pass
- Oral corticosteroids (prednisone, methylprednisolone) — no drug row; generic labels vary by
  manufacturer, none resolved. Short courses treat exacerbations; chronic daily use defines
  OCS-dependent severe asthma for biologic eligibility
- Cut from the table for space (Step 5 eosinophilic add-ons): [mepolizumab/Nucala](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=fefb887c-e4ac-431e-8893-e9d1a5a63fea), [benralizumab/Fasenra](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=da6aca1a-19ed-44a4-abb7-696c7d58b784), [dupilumab/Dupixent](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=595f437d-2729-40bb-9c62-c8ece1f82780) (ages ≥6, OCS-dependent)
- "No boxed warning" status for Nucala, Fasenra, Dupixent, and Tezspire (kept, above) came from
  summarized DailyMed views, not a full read of each complete label — flag for factcheck
- Airsupra's Sept 2025 label-expansion claim is Tier 3 (company source) only
- Nucala's COPD indication and Tezspire's Oct 2025 CRSwNP indication are real but not asthma —
  excluded from scope
- Measured US SABA-only prescribing rates — not available; the GINA-vs-NAEPP gap above compares
  two guideline texts, not observed practice
