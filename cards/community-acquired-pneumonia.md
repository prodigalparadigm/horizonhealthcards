# Community-acquired pneumonia — Adult (ICD-10 J18.9)

## Quick Summary
Acute pulmonary infection acquired outside the healthcare setting; a leading infectious cause of hospitalization and death in older and comorbid adults. Presents with cough, fever, dyspnea, pleuritic pain, and a new infiltrate on imaging. Site-of-care and severity are set by PSI (preferred over CURB-65, 2019 ATS/IDSA, strong) or the 9-criteria minor/2-criteria major severe-CAP definition; the 2019 guideline retired HCAP as a category. Empiric antibacterials are chosen by site of care and comorbidities, with culture-guided de-escalation at 48 hours.

[CID](https://academic.oup.com/cid/article/82/4/625/8364680)
- **2025-07-18** — **2025 ATS update (ATS only — IDSA is not a co-sponsor)**, *AJRCCM* 212(1):24-44, makes four changes to the 2019 ATS/IDSA baseline: (1) lung ultrasound conditionally acceptable as a CXR alternative where operator expertise exists; (2) **against** empiric antibacterials in outpatients without comorbidities who test virus-positive (conditional; comorbid/hospitalized patients still get them); (3) duration for outpatients/non-severe inpatients reaching early stability shortened to **under 5 days, minimum 3** (conditional), vs. severe CAP **5+ days** (strong); (4) corticosteroids **against** in non-severe hospitalized CAP (strong), **for** in severe hospitalized CAP (conditional, low-quality evidence). Everything not addressed here still follows 2019. [AJRCCM 212(1):24-44](https://academic.oup.com/ajrccm/article/212/1/24/8435770)
- **2025-11-11** — ATS press release on the steroid and duration recommendations above. [ATS](https://site.thoracic.org/press-releases/treatment-with-antibiotics-and-steroids-in-adults-with-pneumonia-addressed-in-latest-clinical-practice-guideline)
